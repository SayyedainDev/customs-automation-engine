Transit trade is when goods pass through Pakistan on their way to or from
another country, without actually being sold or consumed in Pakistan itself.
Because the goods are not entering the Pakistani market, they are generally
not subject to Pakistani import duty - the whole point is to move through
the country, under customs control, to their real destination.

The best-known example is Afghan Transit Trade, an arrangement that lets
goods bound for landlocked Afghanistan move through Pakistani ports and
across the border under a bonded transit process, since Afghanistan has no
sea port of its own. The goods are tracked and bonded the whole way to make
sure they actually leave Pakistan rather than being diverted into the local
market untaxed.

Because transit goods bypass normal import duty, this movement is more
tightly monitored than an ordinary import - typically involving bonded
transport, tracking, and guarantees that the goods actually cross the border
as declared.
