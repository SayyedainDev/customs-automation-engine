question: What is a bill of lading?

A bill of lading is issued by the shipping line when your cargo is loaded
onto a vessel. It does three jobs at once: it is a receipt proving the
carrier actually has your goods, it is evidence of the contract for carrying
them, and - unless marked otherwise - it can act as a document of title,
meaning whoever legally holds it can claim the goods at the other end.

That third role is why banks handling letters of credit usually insist on
seeing the original bill of lading before releasing payment: it is the
document that proves who controls the goods.

For air freight, the equivalent document is called an airway bill instead,
and it works differently - an airway bill is only a receipt and contract of
carriage, not a document of title, so whoever is named as the consignee
receives the goods directly rather than whoever is physically holding the
paper.
