A preferential tariff is a reduced customs duty rate available under a trade
agreement between Pakistan and another country or bloc, instead of the
standard rate that would otherwise apply. Pakistan has several such
arrangements, including free trade agreements with specific countries,
regional agreements like SAFTA, and preferential access schemes like the
EU's GSP+.

You do not get the preferential rate automatically just because the goods
are physically shipped from Pakistan - you have to prove the goods actually
qualify as "originating" from Pakistan under that specific agreement's rules
of origin. Rules of origin typically require that the goods were either
wholly produced in Pakistan, or that enough of the manufacturing or value
was added there, depending on the exact test the agreement uses.

Proving origin is usually done with a Certificate of Origin issued for that
specific agreement - a generic Certificate of Origin is not always
sufficient, since different agreements can have different origin
certificates and criteria. It is worth checking which agreement your buyer's
country is a party to, and which specific certificate it requires, well
before shipping.
