A Certificate of Origin is a document that states which country your goods
were actually made in - not which country you shipped them from. Customs in
the destination country uses it to decide how to treat your goods.

The reason it matters so much is trade agreements. If Pakistan has a
preferential trade deal with the country you are exporting to, a valid
Certificate of Origin can qualify your goods for a lower import duty there.
Without it, the buyer's customs authority may charge the full standard rate
instead.

In Pakistan, these certificates are typically issued through the Trade
Development Authority of Pakistan (TDAP) or a recognised chamber of commerce,
often electronically through the Pakistan Single Window. You will usually
need to show proof of where the goods were produced or manufactured, such as
your invoice and production records.

A Certificate of Origin is not the same as a Certificate of Origin form
required for GSP or a free trade agreement specifically - some destinations
ask for a particular version tied to the trade agreement being used, so it is
worth checking what the buyer's country actually requires.
