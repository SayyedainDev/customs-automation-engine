Duty drawback is a refund of the customs duty you already paid on imported
raw materials or components, given back to you after you use them to
manufacture goods that are then exported. The idea is that Pakistan does not
want to effectively tax exports by taxing the imported inputs that went into
making them.

It works differently from bonded-warehouse duty suspension: with drawback,
you pay the import duty first, then claim a refund afterward once you can
show the material was used in goods that were actually exported. The
refund rates are set out in specific schedules for particular product
categories - the textile sector, for example, has its own duty drawback
schedule.

To claim drawback you generally need to show a clear paper trail linking the
imported material to the exported finished product, which is why keeping
consistent records across your import and export declarations matters if you
plan to claim it.
