A letter of credit is a promise from the buyer's bank that it will pay you,
the exporter, once you present the documents proving you shipped the goods
as agreed - typically your commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of
lading or airway bill. It shifts the payment risk from "trusting the buyer to
pay" to "trusting the buyer's bank to pay".

This is especially useful when you and the buyer have not worked together
before, or when the buyer's country carries more payment risk. As long as
your documents match exactly what the letter of credit specifies, the bank is
obliged to pay - which is also why exporters need to be careful that every
document is consistent and correctly worded, since even small mismatches can
delay payment.

An "irrevocable" letter of credit, the most common kind used in Pakistani
exports, cannot be cancelled or changed by the buyer without your agreement
once it has been issued.
