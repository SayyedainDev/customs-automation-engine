Incoterms are a standard set of three-letter trade terms that spell out
exactly who is responsible for what during a shipment - who pays for
freight, who insures the goods, and at what point risk passes from seller to
buyer. They are internationally recognised, so writing "FOB" or "CIF" on a
contract means roughly the same thing anywhere in the world.

FOB (Free On Board) means the seller's responsibility ends once the goods
are loaded onto the ship at the port of origin - from that point on, the
buyer arranges and pays for freight, insurance, and everything else. CIF
(Cost, Insurance and Freight) means the seller pays for shipping and
insurance to the destination port, though risk still technically passes to
the buyer once the goods are loaded, even though the seller is paying the
onward costs.

Which term you use affects your invoice value too: a CIF price includes
freight and insurance, while an FOB price does not, so the same goods can
show a different declared value depending on which Incoterm was used - it is
worth being clear about which one you are quoting and declaring consistently
across your documents.
