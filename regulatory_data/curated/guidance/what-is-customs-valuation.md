question: How does customs decide what my goods are worth?

Since duty is usually charged as a percentage of value, customs needs a
reliable way to decide what your goods are actually worth. The starting
point, and the method customs is generally supposed to use first, is the
"transaction value" - simply the price you actually paid or agreed to pay for
the goods, as shown on your commercial invoice.

Customs will not just accept any number, though. If the declared value looks
unusually low compared to what similar goods normally sell for, or the
relationship between buyer and seller might have affected the price (for
example, if they are related companies), customs can look further: comparing
it to the value of identical or similar goods imported before, or building
the value up from known costs.

Pakistan Customs also issues valuation rulings for specific product
categories, which set a reference value customs will check declarations
against. If your invoice value differs a lot from a relevant valuation
ruling, expect that to be questioned - it is worth checking whether one
exists for your product before you ship.
