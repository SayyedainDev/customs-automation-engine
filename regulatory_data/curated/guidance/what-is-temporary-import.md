Temporary import covers goods brought into Pakistan for a limited purpose
and a limited time, with the intention of taking them back out again rather
than selling them locally - things like exhibition displays, professional
equipment, or machinery brought in for a specific job. Because the goods are
meant to leave again, full import duty is often not charged, sometimes
against a bond or guarantee that is released once the goods are re-exported.

An ATA Carnet is an internationally recognised document that makes this
easier across many countries at once: it acts as a temporary passport for
the goods, letting them move in and out of participating countries without
needing a separate customs bond or full declaration at every border,
provided they leave again within the carnet's validity period.

Temporary import is different from a straightforward re-export or transit
shipment: with temporary import, the goods are actually used or displayed
within Pakistan for a while, they just are not meant to stay permanently -
so the exact requirements around what the goods are used for, and proof they
were re-exported afterward, matter for the duty treatment to hold.
