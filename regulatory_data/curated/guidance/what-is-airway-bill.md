question: What is an airway bill and how is it different from a bill of lading?

An airway bill is issued by an airline (or its agent) when your cargo is
accepted for air freight. Like a bill of lading, it is a receipt for the
goods and evidence of the contract to carry them.

The key difference is that an airway bill is never a document of title. A
sea bill of lading can be transferred to someone else, who then has the
right to claim the goods; an airway bill cannot. Whoever is named as the
consignee on the airway bill is who the airline releases the goods to,
directly, without needing to physically present the original document.

This makes air freight faster to release at the destination, which is one
reason it is often used for smaller, higher-value, or time-sensitive
shipments, alongside its speed of transit.
