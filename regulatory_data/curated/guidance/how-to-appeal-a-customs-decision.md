If you disagree with a customs decision - a duty assessment, a valuation, a
penalty, or a seizure - the Customs Act sets out a formal appeal process
rather than leaving it to informal negotiation with the officer involved.

The general path is: the decision is first made by the customs officer or
collector handling your case, and if you disagree you can generally appeal
to a higher customs authority, and beyond that to the Customs Appellate
Tribunal, which is independent of Pakistan Customs itself. There are also
formal timelines for filing an appeal after a decision is issued, so
delaying too long can mean losing the right to challenge it.

Separately from a formal appeal, the FBR and Pakistan Customs also have
complaint channels for service issues - delays, conduct, or process
problems that are not really about the legal correctness of a decision.
Because appeal timelines and the exact procedure matter a lot here, this is
an area worth getting a customs agent or someone familiar with the current
process involved early rather than after time limits have passed.
