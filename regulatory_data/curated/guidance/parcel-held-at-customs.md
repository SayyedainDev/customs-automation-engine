A parcel being held is usually not a sign anything is wrong - it generally
means customs wants more information before releasing it. Common reasons
include: duty and tax being owed that has not been paid yet, the declared
value or contents looking inconsistent with what was actually sent, a
missing invoice or description, or the item falling into a restricted
category that needs an extra permit.

The courier or postal service handling the parcel (or the customs broker
they use) is usually the first point of contact - they can tell you exactly
why it is being held and what is needed to release it, since they can see
the specific case in the system. If duty is owed, you will typically need to
pay it before release; if a document or explanation is missing, providing it
promptly is usually what moves things along.

If you believe a charge or hold is genuinely wrong - not just unwelcome, but
incorrect - Pakistan Customs has a formal complaint and appeal process you
can use, rather than only relying on the courier to sort it out informally.
