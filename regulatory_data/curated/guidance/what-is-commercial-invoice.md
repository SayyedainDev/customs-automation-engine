question: What is a commercial invoice and why does customs need it?

A commercial invoice is the seller's bill for the goods being shipped - it
lists what is being sold, how much of it, the price, who is buying it, and
who is selling it. It is not the same as a packing list, which describes how
the goods are physically packed rather than what they cost.

Customs relies on the commercial invoice as the starting point for working
out how much duty and tax is owed, because duty is usually calculated as a
percentage of the value of the goods. If the invoice value looks wrong or
inconsistent with the goods described, customs can ask for more evidence of
the real transaction value before releasing the shipment.

A good commercial invoice states the buyer and seller clearly, a description
of the goods that matches what is actually in the shipment, the currency and
total value, and the terms of sale (an Incoterm such as FOB or CIF). Vague or
inconsistent descriptions are one of the most common reasons a shipment gets
held for extra checks.
