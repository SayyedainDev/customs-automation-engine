The Export Facilitation Scheme lets exporters import the raw materials and
inputs they need for making export goods without paying duty and sales tax
upfront, instead of paying first and claiming it back later through duty
drawback. It replaced several older, separate schemes that worked similarly
for different sectors, bringing them under one unified framework.

To use it, you generally need to be registered under the scheme and show
that the imported inputs are actually going into goods you will export,
often tracked through input-output ratios that set how much imported
material is reasonably expected to go into a given quantity of export
output.

It exists for the same underlying reason as duty drawback - so Pakistani
exporters are not paying import duty on materials that ultimately leave the
country again as finished exports - but by removing the tax upfront rather
than refunding it afterward, it avoids tying up an exporter's cash flow
while waiting for a drawback claim to be processed.
