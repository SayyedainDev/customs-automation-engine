Once a Goods Declaration is filed, it goes through a few general stages
before your goods are released. First, the system assesses the
declaration - checking the HS code, calculating the duty and taxes owed
based on the declared value, and deciding, usually based on risk factors, how
much scrutiny the shipment needs.

Depending on that risk assessment, a shipment might go straight through with
only a documents check, or it might be selected for physical examination,
where an officer actually opens and inspects the cargo against what was
declared. If everything matches and duty and taxes are paid, customs issues
clearance and the goods can be released from the port or airport.

If something does not match - the goods do not match the description, the
value looks understated, or a required document is missing - the shipment
can be held for further review rather than cleared immediately. That does
not necessarily mean anything is wrong; it usually just means customs wants
more evidence before releasing it, and resolving it typically means providing
the requested documents or explanation to the officer handling the case.
