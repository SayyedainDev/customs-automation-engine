SRO stands for Statutory Regulatory Order. It is how the Pakistani
government issues detailed rules, rates, and exemptions under the authority
of a parent law - like the Customs Act - without needing to pass a whole new
act of parliament every time a rate or condition needs to change.

This is why so much of the practical detail in Pakistani customs and trade -
specific duty exemptions, scheme rules like the Export Facilitation Scheme,
or amendments to the Export Policy Order - lives in SROs rather than in the
Customs Act itself. The Act sets the overall legal framework; SROs fill in
and update the specifics underneath it, and can be issued or amended much
more quickly than the Act itself.

Each SRO is numbered and dated - for example "SRO 957(I)/2021" - and it is
common for one SRO to later be amended by another SRO, so when checking a
rule it matters whether you are looking at the original SRO or the latest
amended version.
