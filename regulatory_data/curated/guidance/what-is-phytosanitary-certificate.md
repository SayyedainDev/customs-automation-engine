question: What is a phytosanitary certificate and when do I need one?
topics: [documents, agriculture, export, import]

A phytosanitary certificate confirms that plant material - crops, seeds,
raw cotton, wood packaging, and similar agricultural goods - has been
inspected and is free from pests and plant diseases that could spread to the
destination country's agriculture. It is issued by the plant quarantine
authority, not by customs itself, though customs will check that it is
present before releasing a shipment that needs one.

You generally need one whenever you are exporting or importing goods of
plant origin, or sometimes wood packaging material used to crate other
goods. Many countries will refuse entry to agricultural shipments that lack
a valid phytosanitary certificate, regardless of how correct the rest of the
paperwork is, because the concern is biological risk rather than value or
classification.

If your product is not obviously agricultural but does involve any plant
material - wooden pallets or crates, for instance - it is worth checking
whether a phytosanitary certificate applies to the packaging even if it
does not apply to the goods themselves.
